# Emma Kunz — Artist Biography

**Born**: 23 May 1892, Brittnau, Canton of Aargau, Switzerland  
**Died**: 16 January 1963, Waldstatt, Appenzell Ausserrhoden, Switzerland  
**Also known as**: Penta (nickname)

## Overview

Emma Kunz was a Swiss healer, researcher, and artist whose life bridged rural Swiss folk medicine, spiritualism, and a rigorous personal cosmology expressed through hundreds of geometric drawings. She never received formal art training and did not consider herself an artist in the conventional sense. Her drawings were created as part of a healing and divinatory practice using **radiesthesia** — a technique in which a divining pendulum guides the placement of lines on graph paper.

Today Kunz is recognized as a significant figure in 20th-century art history, often discussed alongside Hilma af Klint and Agnes Martin in exhibitions exploring spiritual abstraction and outsider art. Her work has been shown at the Venice Biennale, Serpentine Galleries, Aargauer Kunsthaus, and museums worldwide.

## Early Life

Kunz was born into poverty; her father Stephan Oswald Kunz was a hand weaver. Three siblings died in childhood. When Emma was 17, her brother and father both committed suicide — events that shaped her lifelong interest in healing and the restoration of balance.

She discovered telepathy and extra-sensory perception as a child and began using these gifts professionally from age 18. Early drawings were made in exercise books, long before her large-scale works on graph paper.

## Healing Practice

Kunz worked as a naturopath and telepathic healer in rural Switzerland. Patients visited her seeking help for physical and mental ailments. Her drawings served as **diagnostic instruments**: she placed a drawing between herself and the patient during healing sessions, reading energy fields visualized in the geometric compositions.

### Radiesthesia Method

From 1938, while in her forties, Kunz began making large-scale drawings using a divining pendulum to plot complex linear compositions on millimetre or graph paper. Each drawing was typically completed in a single continuous session that could last up to 24 hours. She worked with graphite, coloured pencils, oil crayons, and carpenter's pencils.

Kunz never titled or dated her drawings. Their medical and philosophical meanings were recorded separately in books she maintained throughout her life.

### AION A — The Healing Rock

In 1942 Kunz discovered a Roman quarry grotto in Würenlos, which she named **AION A**. The mineral composition — primarily calcium carbonate and silicic acid from quartz — became central to her healing practice. She used the pendulum to identify its restorative properties.

AION A is still mined from the same quarry and sold in Swiss pharmacies for rheumatic diseases, joint and muscle pain, and inflammatory skin conditions. The site is now home to the **Emma Kunz Zentrum** and Grotto.

### Other Research

Kunz also used radiesthesia to polarise marigolds in her garden, producing multiple flower heads, and documented the results with photographs. She published three books covering poetry, telepathy, and prophecy.

## Artistic Output

Kunz produced over 400 large-scale geometric drawings between 1938 and 1963. The complete oeuvre is catalogued in 581 entries across three series:

- **Kreis (Circle)**: 92 works — concentric and radial geometries
- **Kreuz (Cross)**: 172 works — cruciform and bound-cross motifs
- **Komplex (Complex)**: 316 works — multi-layered lattice and mandala structures

Kunz predicted that her drawings were destined for the 21st century — a prophecy fulfilled by the surge of institutional interest since the 1970s.

## Posthumous Recognition

Kunz's work was first exhibited publicly in **1973–74** at the Aargauer Kunsthaus (*Der Fall Emma Kunz*), curated by Harald Szeemann. Her relative Anton C. Meier safeguarded her estate and founded the **Emma Kunz Zentrum** in Würenlos in 1986.

## Legacy & Institutions

- **Emma Kunz Zentrum / Foundation** (Würenlos): Primary archive, 581-work catalogue, AION A grotto
- **Emma Kunz Museum** (Würenlos): Permanent collection display
- **Aargauer Kunsthaus** (Aarau): 500+ works, online filterable catalogue
- **Anton Meier Collection**: Provenance chain from Kunz's estate through Otto Kunz to Meier
