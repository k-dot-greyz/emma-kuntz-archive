## Significance

This was Emma Kunz's **first solo exhibition in the United Kingdom** and her first solo presentation since the 1970s Aargauer Kunsthaus show. It introduced Kunz's healing-based drawing practice to a broad international audience at a moment when art institutions were increasingly receptive to spiritual, meditative, and self-taught practices previously excluded from art history.

Curator Melissa Blanchflower noted: *"We're in a climate where art is much more porous and people are embracing work that has been written off by the history books. There's an increasing appetite for different forms of meditative practice and work that makes us ponder the equilibrium of the cosmos."*

## Exhibition Concept

The Serpentine presentation was not a conventional retrospective. Christodoulos Panayiotou's involvement shaped the exhibition as an **encounter** rather than a display:

### Panayiotou's AION A Benches

Panayiotou produced stone benches quarried from the AION A healing rock in Würenlos — the same mineral Kunz discovered in 1942 and used in her naturopathic practice. Visitors could sit on these benches while viewing the drawings, collapsing the distance between Kunz's healing ritual (where drawings were placed between healer and patient) and the museum viewing experience.

### Spatial Logic

Kunz's drawings were originally used in healing sessions — placed between herself and the patient, not hung on walls for contemplation. The Serpentine installation acknowledged this by creating an environment where the drawings functioned as energy fields in a shared space rather than isolated objects on white walls.

## Works Featured

The exhibition drew primarily from the Emma Kunz Zentrum collection. Approximately 65 drawings were selected, spanning Kunz's output from 1938 to 1963. None were titled or dated by the artist; curatorial identification relies on the Foundation's catalogue system.

## Tour — Muzeum Susch

Following London, the exhibition travelled to Muzeum Susch in the Engadin valley, Switzerland (26 July – 10 November 2019), anchoring the international presentation back in Kunz's home country.

## Critical Reception

The exhibition generated significant press coverage, including features in *The Art Newspaper*, *Frieze*, and *Artforum*. Key themes in critical response:

1. **Repositioning outsider art** — Kunz's work as healing instrument vs. fine art object
2. **Feminist art history** — parallels with Hilma af Klint's recently rediscovered oeuvre
3. **Contemporary relevance** — meditative practice, energy fields, and cosmic equilibrium in 21st-century culture
4. **Institutional ethics** — presenting healing rituals within white-cube gallery contexts

## Impact on This Archive

The Serpentine 2019 exhibition is the primary source for high-resolution press images of several works in this collection (Work No. 012, 396). The exhibition's catalogue and online documentation provide the most accessible English-language scholarship on Kunz's practice.
